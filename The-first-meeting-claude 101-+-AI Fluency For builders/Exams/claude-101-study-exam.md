# Claude 101 — Study Group Exam

**Grounded in:** the official curriculum for [Claude 101](https://anthropic.skilljar.com/claude-101) on Anthropic's course platform, cross-checked against how Projects, Artifacts, Skills, Connectors, Enterprise Search, and Research actually work today.

**Honest note on method:** I can't watch the lesson videos themselves — only the public syllabus is visible without enrolling. So this isn't a transcript quiz; it's built from the real, official lesson-by-lesson outline plus verified, current knowledge of each feature. If something here doesn't match what you actually saw in a lesson, that mismatch is worth flagging on your call — it means either the course has more nuance than the syllabus shows, or one of you remembers it differently. Both are useful discussion.

**Course shape, for reference:**
1. **Meet Claude** — What is Claude? · Your first conversation · Getting better results · Working with Claude on desktop
2. **Organizing your work and knowledge** — Projects · Artifacts · Skills
3. **Expanding Claude's reach** — Connecting your tools · Enterprise search · Research
4. **Putting it all together** — Claude in action: use-cases by role · Other ways to work with Claude
5. **Conclusion & certificate**

**How to run this with your group:** everyone solves solo first, no peeking at the key — budget 45–60 min. Sections A–E are objective; score them 1 point each (42 pts total) and compare numbers. Sections F and G don't have one right answer — that's where you should spend the most time on the call, since it's the closest thing here to how a client conversation actually feels.

### Section F — discussion guide
43. Set up a shared **Project** per client with brand guidelines, tone notes, and past campaigns loaded in as context; add a **Skill** for "write in this client's voice" so the standard is repeatable, not tribal knowledge sitting with one account lead.
44. Load the contract once into a **Project** (or point a **Connector** at wherever it's stored) so everyone works from the same context instead of re-pasting. The real cost isn't just time — it's inconsistent answers on a legal document, which is a compliance risk, not just an inconvenience.
45. **Artifacts** — it gives them a standalone, versioned pane for that one-pager that's easy to keep editing and exporting, instead of digging through chat history.
46. **Enterprise search**. Flag clearly: it only surfaces what's *already connected*, access follows each person's own existing permissions (no backdoor access), and an admin has to deliberately enable each source — so it's worth an explicit conversation about what should and shouldn't be connected before flipping it on.
47. **Research** — it's built for multi-step, cited investigation. The habit to insist on: always open and skim the actual cited sources before repeating a claim in a board deck; treat citations as a starting point for verification, not proof by themselves.
48. No — different roles hit different bottlenecks (a salesperson drafting outreach vs. an engineer debugging), so effective training shows role-relevant examples even while teaching the same underlying skills (Projects, Artifacts, good prompting).
49. Recommend a **Project** for the wedding — same underlying value as the business case: centralizing scattered context (budget, vendors, decisions) so nothing gets lost or contradicted across chats. Organization problems don't care whether the client is a company or a person.
50. Strong answers usually mention: (1) some baseline training so habits are consistent, not accidental; (2) a couple of light guardrails or examples of good use; (3) a way to collect what's working/not working early; (4) revisiting after 2–4 weeks rather than "set and forget."

### Section G
51. A Project is a *workspace* holding ongoing context across many chats; an Artifact is a *single piece of content* you're iterating on.
52. Any two of: desktop app, mobile app, API, browser extension/other integrations.
53. Open — but strong answers usually name Projects (biggest habit/consistency win for teams) or "getting better results" prompting basics (foundational, applies everywhere) and defend the choice against the 5-minute constraint.
